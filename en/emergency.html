When to call us

Common dental emergencies

Severe pain

Persistent or acute toothache that does not ease with a painkiller.

Broken tooth

A fracture, crack or tooth knocked out after an impact.

Swelling / abscess

Swelling in the face or gums, a sign of possible infection.

Bleeding

Bleeding that does not stop after an extraction or injury.

Loose restoration

A filling, crown or cap that has come off or broken.

Injury

A blow to the mouth or teeth, especially in children.

Until we are in touch

What you can do in the meantime

01

For pain

Rinse gently with lukewarm salt water and take a common painkiller according to its instructions. Avoid placing aspirin directly on the gum.

02

For a knocked-out tooth

Hold it by the crown (not the root), rinse it gently and keep it in a little milk or saliva. Contact us as soon as possible — time matters.

03

For swelling

Apply a cold compress to the outside of the face for 10-15 minutes. Do not heat the area.

04

For bleeding

Press gently with clean gauze for 10-15 minutes. If it continues heavily, contact us or the nearest health centre.

The above are general first-aid guidelines and do not replace medical assessment. In case of serious injury, difficulty breathing or swallowing, call 166 (Greek ambulance service) or 112 immediately.
